# Part VI
# Sector Playbooks and Proof
## Introduction

---

Parts I through V built a universal migration architecture — threat framing, standards literacy, cryptographic inventory, implementation patterns, and programme governance. Part VI answers the question every practitioner asks when the universal model meets their employer: **what changes in banking, defence, energy, or cloud — and how do we prove the programme works?**

Universal frameworks without sector overlays produce programmes that are technically correct and operationally rejected. A DORA-regulated bank cannot sequence migration like a SaaS platform. A gas transmission operator cannot treat firmware signing like corporate TLS. A defence contractor cannot merge NSS and commercial evidence zones without assessor findings. Part VI provides **sector playbooks** that modify TRADE weights, HLM timelines, procurement emphasis, and assurance evidence — without duplicating the programme machinery Parts I–V already defined.

Part VI is also **proof**: the four teaching organisations introduced in Part I return with full programme arcs, Year 2–3 results, supervisory and assessor outcomes, and honest lessons learned. Readers who followed Meridian, Northfield, Apex, or GlobalSync through earlier chapters will see how inventory, architecture, governance, and assurance converge under sector constraints.

---

## What Part VI Delivers

Part VI comprises four chapters progressing from sector-specific execution patterns to sustained organisational capability:

**Chapter 19 — Financial Services Playbook** applies the universal model to banking and payments — DORA supervisory evidence, PCI and payment HSM certification cycles, SWIFT and market infrastructure dependencies, and the Sector Overlay Matrix (SOM) financial services modifier. Meridian Mutual Bank's programme arc from initial DORA briefing through Year 2 examination results provides the teaching thread.

**Chapter 20 — Defense, Government, and Critical Infrastructure** addresses NSS, defence industrial base, and OT-heavy operators — CNSA 2.0 floors, CMMC and FedRAMP evidence separation, NERC CIP integration, and long-lived asset constraints. Apex Defense Technologies and Northfield Energy Systems run parallel threads illustrating classified/commercial boundaries and OT/IT convergence.

**Chapter 21 — Cloud, SaaS, and Multinational Compliance** covers tenant-isolated PQC architectures, shared responsibility models, cross-border evidence under GDPR, and platform concentration risk. GlobalSync Logistics' three-region programme demonstrates EU/US regulatory split without duplicate programme offices.

**Chapter 22 — Sustaining Quantum Resilience** closes the book — standards watch, algorithm contingency planning, PQ-ADAPT Level 5 operations, and a Year 3 retrospective across all four organisations. Migration ends in capability, not in a single deployment wave.

Together, these chapters complete the **Proof** phase of ARCS and advance enterprises from PQ-ADAPT Level 4 (*Transitioning*) toward Level 5 (*Quantum-Resilient*).

## What Part VI Is and Is Not

Part VI is **sector proof** — how universal migration architecture survives banking supervision, defence assessment, OT field operations, and multinational cloud regulation.

It is **not** a replacement for Parts I–V. Sector playbooks annotate the universal model; they do not redefine algorithm standards, CBOM methodology, or programme governance.

It is **not** exhaustive sector coverage. Healthcare, telecommunications, and public-sector civilian agencies receive SOM principles applicable by analogy; dedicated healthcare expansion remains future work.

It is **not** vendor product guidance. Cloud provider names appear as **categories** for assessment frameworks — no product endorsement.

It **is** the closing argument that enterprise post-quantum migration is achievable, measurable, and defensible when sector constraints are honoured rather than ignored.
