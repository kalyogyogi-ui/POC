# Part V
# Running the Migration Programme
## Introduction

---

Parts I through IV answered *why*, *what*, *where*, and *how*. Part V answers **who governs the migration, when decisions get made, and how the programme sustains momentum** after the first wave plan receives board approval.

Architecture standards without programme authority produce excellent documents and inconsistent adoption. Procurement without contractual teeth produces vendor questionnaires that claim PQC readiness while production systems remain classical-only. CI/CD pipelines without governance gates merge quantum-vulnerable dependencies at scale. Assurance without programme integration produces penetration test findings that sit in ticket backlogs while steering committees hear green status slides.

Part V is where post-quantum migration becomes **operational enterprise discipline** — not a security project with an end date.

---

## What Part V Delivers

Part V comprises four chapters addressing the programme machinery that Parts I–IV assume but do not fully specify:

**Chapter 15 — Programme Governance and Operating Model** develops the PQC Governance Stack (Chapter 3) into a functioning programme office: charter, RACI, steering committee rhythm, KPI dashboards, board reporting, and funding models. Without migration authority, pilots become permanent exceptions.

**Chapter 16 — Procurement, Contracts, and Third-Party Risk** encodes PQC requirements in commercial relationships — contractual clauses, vendor evidence requests, DORA third-party ICT risk integration, and supplier CBOM obligations. Third-party cryptography is your cryptography; contracts are enforcement mechanisms.

**Chapter 17 — Software Supply Chain and Embedded Cryptography** integrates PQC migration with SBOM/CBOM pipelines, CI/CD verification gates, and dependency management — where most enterprise cryptography actually lives.

**Chapter 18 — FIPS Validation, Testing, and Assurance** defines the assurance programme for regulated environments: FIPS 140-3 module transitions, interoperability testing, red team considerations, and examination evidence packages.

Together, these chapters complete the **Synchronize** phase of ARCS and advance enterprises from PQ-ADAPT Level 3 (*Architected*) toward Level 4 (*Transitioning*).

## What Part V Is and Is Not

Part V is **programme execution governance** — authority, rhythm, procurement, supply chain, and assurance machinery.

It is **not** a project management methodology textbook. PRINCE2, SAFe, and ITIL integrate at the edges; PQC programme specifics are the focus.

It is **not** sector-specific playbook content — banking, defence, and energy specialisations are Part VI.

It is **not** cryptographic implementation — architecture patterns are Part IV.

It **is** the organisational layer that prevents Parts I–IV artefacts from decaying into shelfware.
